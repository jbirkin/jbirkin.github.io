About Me

I'm an AI engineer at Famēlia, a healthtech product used by dietitians and patients to adapt any recipe to a family's dietary needs. I develop Amelia, a Python/LangGraph pipeline served over FastAPI that takes a recipe and a household's restrictions — allergies, medical conditions, preferences — and rewrites it ingredient by ingredient, step by step, for every person eating it. Recent work includes a knowledge-graph representation of recipes, so a substitution propagates correctly through shared cooking steps rather than just the ingredient list; hybrid model routing that sends structured reasoning and user-facing prose to different models; and a retrieval system over a 3,500-page clinical nutrition manual that grounds the dietitian chatbot in a real reference instead of the model's own recall.

Before this I spent seven years in astrophysics research. Originally from near York in the UK, I moved north to study Physics and Astronomy at Durham University, staying on after my Master's in 2018 to complete a PhD under Prof. Ian Smail and Prof. Mark Swinbank, finishing in August 2022. I then spent three years in College Station, Texas, as a Postdoctoral Research Associate at Texas A&M University, studying dusty star-forming galaxies with ALMA, JWST and the VLT. Most of it was data science work with an astronomy theme — Bayesian model fitting on low signal-to-noise data, multi-terabyte processing pipelines, and developing open-source tools for my collaborators. I'm no longer active in the field; my papers, code and data are collected on the astronomy page.

I now live in Maryville, Missouri.
